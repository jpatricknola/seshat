SESHAT VALIDATION SCRIPT — copy-paste version
Source: docs/validation-script.md. Say each prompt to Seshat, check Live, move on.
Remember: OSC is UDP with no reply — "nothing happened" is a failure, write it down.
Before starting: save your work; step 1 opens a fresh set and deletes default tracks.

========== PART 1 — OPEN THE SET ==========

prompt: Let's start a new project. Make me three MIDI tracks: Keys, Bass, and Drums.
result: MIXED: a new project was not opened, three new midi tracks were added to an existing project that was already open with 4 existing tracks.

prompt: What's in the session right now?
result: MIXED: i deleted the 4 existing tracks so that only the 3 midi ones remained.  Seshat responded that there were 7 tracks, so it saw a stale session state that did not reflect the action i took before asking the question.
The clip grid — which is the tool that actually enumerates every track and what's in it, shows only three tracks: Keys, Bass, and Drums.  But that contradicts the session-state readout showing 7 tracks.

prompt: This is a lo-fi thing, take it down to 86 BPM. And put the metronome on while we work.
result: PASS: everything worked as expected.

========== PART 2 — FIND SOUNDS ==========

prompt: Have you got an index of my library yet? If not, build one — I'll wait.
good result: First run: warns it takes up to a minute and Live's UI may freeze, then runs and completes (Live looking hung partway is expected). Second run: says the catalog already exists and skips it.
bad result: Silently freezes Live with no warning, times out, or re-indexes from scratch when a catalog already exists.

prompt: I want a warm, slightly out-of-tune electric piano for the Keys track. What have I got?
good result: Several candidates, each with a one-line reason referencing the musical context (lo-fi, 86 BPM). Names are real presets from your library.
bad result: Loads one result without asking, gives only a single option, reasons are generic name-parroting, or preset names look invented.

prompt: Load the second one.
good result: The device appears on the Keys track in Live's device view, and Seshat confirms by naming what actually landed (which may differ if the URI resolved oddly).
bad result: Nothing appears on Keys, it lands on the wrong track, or Seshat claims a load without naming/verifying the actual device.

prompt: Bass next — something warm and analog, nothing too clicky.
good result: Same shape: options offered, your choice respected, device lands on the Bass track.
bad result: Loads without offering options, or the device lands on Keys (or anywhere other than Bass).

prompt: For drums I want a dusty, vinyl-ish kit.
good result: A drum rack or kit preset loaded onto the Drums track.
bad result: Nothing loads, a non-drum instrument loads, or it lands on the wrong track.

prompt: Actually, search Live's browser directly for anything called 'Operator' — I want to see the raw browser results.
good result: Bypasses the catalog and hits Live's browser live. Returns Operator and/or its presets with folder paths. Noticeably slower than catalog search (that's the tell it's a real round-trip).
bad result: Answers instantly from the catalog instead of the live browser, returns nothing, or times out.

prompt: What's on each of my three tracks now?
good result: The device chain per track, in order, matching what you see in Live when you click each track.
bad result: Chains missing, out of order, or not matching the screen.

========== PART 3 — THE FIRST IDEA ==========

prompt: Give me a four-bar chord progression on Keys — something moody in F minor. Lay it in the first scene.
good result: A clip appears in Keys, scene 1. Open it: four bars long (16 beats at 4/4), notes stacked into chords, and actually in F minor.
bad result: No clip, wrong slot, wrong length, a single melodic line instead of chords, or wrong key.

prompt: Show me the clip grid — what's where?
good result: Three tracks with their types (all MIDI), scene names, and only the Keys clip listed as occupied — matching the Session view slot for slot.
bad result: Grid doesn't match the screen, track types missing, or empty slots reported as occupied (or vice versa).

prompt: Play it.
good result: Clip fires, transport rolls, you hear the chords on the electric piano you picked, at 86 BPM.
bad result: Nothing plays, wrong clip fires, or Seshat claims playback with no sound.

prompt: Stop.
good result: Playback stops.
bad result: Keeps playing, or only partially stops.

========== PART 4 — BASS AND DRUMS ==========

prompt: Now a bassline under those chords. Keep it simple — roots, mostly on the downbeats, and leave some space.
good result: Seshat reads the Keys clip first (and says so), then writes a bass clip into scene 1 on Bass. Notes follow the chord roots, in a sensible low octave.
bad result: Guesses the progression without reading the Keys clip, writes to the wrong track/slot, or the bass sits up in the chord register.

prompt: Drums — a laid-back boom-bap pattern, one bar, and loop it.
good result: A one-bar drum clip on Drums. Kick, snare, hats on standard GM drum pitches (C1 kick, D1 snare, F#1 hat) so the drum rack actually sounds.
bad result: Notes land on silent empty pads, pattern isn't one bar, or no clip appears.

prompt: Play the whole first scene.
good result: All three clips launch together and it sounds like a piece of music.
bad result: Only some clips fire, or nothing launches.

prompt: Stop just the drums.
good result: Drums clip stops; Keys and Bass keep playing.
bad result: Everything stops, the wrong track stops, or drums keep going.

prompt: Alright, stop everything.
good result: Full stop.
bad result: Something keeps playing.

========== PART 5 — EDITING WHAT'S ALREADY THERE ==========
(Watch note counts carefully — this is where silent data loss happens.)

prompt: Read me back what's in the Keys clip.
good result: Every note listed with a note name (not just a MIDI number), start beat, length, and velocity. Spot-check three notes against the clip editor — they match, and the note count matches.
bad result: Raw MIDI numbers only, wrong beats/durations, or a note count that doesn't match the screen.

prompt: Those chords are too stiff. Humanize the velocities a bit — some softer, some accented — but don't change any pitches or timing.
good result: Velocities now varied; same number of notes; pitches and timing identical to before.
bad result: THE TRAP: a lazy implementation rewrites the clip and loses the voicing — note count changes, pitches shift, or timing moves.

prompt: Take the bass down an octave.
good result: Bass notes moved down exactly 12 semitones. Note count and rhythm unchanged. Sounds lower, not broken.
bad result: Wrong interval, notes lost, rhythm changed, or the clip rewritten from scratch.

prompt: There's too much going on in bar 3 of the drums — clear out the hats in that bar only.
good result: Surgical removal: hats gone from bar 3 only; kick and snare in bar 3 untouched; bars 1, 2, 4 untouched.
bad result: All hats removed everywhere, other instruments removed, or the whole bar/clip cleared.

prompt: Hmm, undo that.
good result: The hats come back.
bad result: Nothing restored, or the wrong edit is undone.

prompt: No, I was right the first time — redo.
good result: Hats gone again.
bad result: Redo does nothing, or redoes something else.

========== PART 6 — BUILDING AN ARRANGEMENT ==========

prompt: Call this first scene 'Verse'.
good result: Scene 1 renamed in the Session view master column.
bad result: No rename visible, or the wrong scene renamed.

prompt: Duplicate the Verse so I've got somewhere to build a chorus, and name the new one 'Chorus'.
good result: A second scene named Chorus, containing copies of all three clips.
bad result: Empty duplicate, missing clips, wrong name, or no new scene.

prompt: In the Chorus, make the chords bigger — add a higher voicing on top. Leave the Verse alone.
good result: The Chorus Keys clip has added notes; the Verse Keys clip is byte-for-byte untouched (open both and compare — the Verse half is the important half).
bad result: The Verse clip changes too, or the wrong scene's clip is edited.

prompt: Name that clip 'Chorus keys'.
good result: The clip title shows in its Session view slot.
bad result: No rename, or the wrong clip renamed.

prompt: Add an empty scene at the end for a breakdown, and call it 'Break'.
good result: Scene 3 exists, named Break, all slots empty.
bad result: Scene inserted in the wrong place, not empty, or wrong name.

prompt: Copy the Verse bass into the Break — I just want bass there on its own.
good result: The bass clip appears in the Break row; other Break slots stay empty.
bad result: Wrong clip copied, wrong destination, or extra clips appear.

prompt: Show me the grid again.
good result: Three scenes correctly named; Verse and Chorus full, Break holding only bass; clip names reported (including "Chorus keys"). Every cell matches the screen.
bad result: Any cell mismatching the screen, missing scene names, or missing clip names.

prompt: Play the Chorus.
good result: Scene 2 launches, all three clips.
bad result: Wrong scene fires, or only some clips launch.

prompt: Now the Break.
good result: Scene 3 launches; only bass sounds.
bad result: Other tracks keep sounding, or the wrong scene fires.

prompt: Select the Verse scene and the Keys track for me so I can see them.
good result: Selection visibly moves in Live's UI — Keys track highlighted, Verse row selected.
bad result: Selection doesn't move, or the wrong track/scene is selected.

prompt: Stop.
good result: Playback stops.
bad result: Keeps playing.

========== PART 7 — MIXING ==========
(Relative changes — "a bit", "a touch" — require reading current state first.
A jump to a hardcoded value is a failure even if something visibly moved.)

prompt: The drums are too loud. Pull them down a bit.
good result: Seshat reads the current volume first, then the Drums fader moves down a modest amount.
bad result: Fader slammed to zero, reset to a default value, or moves up / not at all.

prompt: Push the keys out to the left a touch, and give the bass dead centre.
good result: Keys pan moves a touch left of centre (not hard left); Bass pan at centre.
bad result: Hard-left pan, wrong track panned, or bass not centred.

prompt: Solo the bass for a second so I can hear it on its own.
good result: Bass solo button lights up.
bad result: Wrong track soloed, or nothing lights.

prompt: OK, unsolo. And mute the keys, I want to hear the rhythm section.
good result: Solo off, Keys muted.
bad result: Solo stays on, wrong track muted.

prompt: Unmute the keys.
good result: Keys back on.
bad result: Still muted, or another track unmuted instead.

prompt: What parameters has the bass instrument got?
good result: A parameter list with names, current values, and min-max ranges, matching Live's device panel.
bad result: Empty list, names not matching the device panel, or ranges missing.

prompt: Close the filter down on the bass — I want it darker.
good result: The filter cutoff knob visibly moves down in Live by a musical fraction of the range, and Seshat reports the new human-readable display value (e.g. "1.2 kHz").
bad result: The wrong knob moves, the cutoff jumps to the minimum, or Seshat reports only a raw normalized number.

prompt: Arm the drums track, I might play something in.
good result: Drums record-arm button lights.
bad result: Wrong track armed, or nothing happens.

prompt: Actually, disarm it.
good result: Arm off.
bad result: Still armed.

prompt: Set the song loop to the first eight bars and turn looping on.
good result: In Arrangement view (press Tab to check): loop brace enabled and spanning bars 1-9. Switch back to Session view after checking.
bad result: Loop not enabled, wrong span, or nothing changes in Arrangement view.

========== PART 8 — MAKING A MESS AND CLEANING IT UP ==========
(These steps check that failures fail LOUDLY.)

prompt: Add an audio track called Vocals.
good result: New audio track named Vocals, at the end.
bad result: MIDI track created instead, wrong position, or wrong name.

prompt: Write a melody on the Vocals track.
good result: TRAP — MIDI can't go on an audio track. Seshat refuses with an explanation naming the track (ideally before even calling the tool, offering a MIDI track instead; the tool's own error is the backstop).
bad result: Claims success. A phantom success here is a genuine failure whichever layer produced it.

prompt: Write a melody on the group track itself.
(Only if this set has a group track — skip otherwise.)
good result: Refuses, names it as a group track, and offers one of the tracks inside it.
bad result: Write silently dropped and reported as success.

prompt: Try writing notes to track 47.
good result: A clean error, fast — the guard gives up after ~2 seconds and says so. A brief pause is expected.
bad result: A 5-second hang, a crash, or a claimed success.

prompt: Play the Keys clip in the Break scene.
good result: TRAP — the Keys slot in Break is empty, and firing an empty slot is Live's way of STOPPING a track. Seshat refuses, says that slot is empty, and points at stop_clip if stopping was the intent.
bad result: Fires the empty slot (silencing Keys) while reporting a successful launch.

prompt: Duplicate the Keys track.
good result: A copy of Keys appears, with its clips and its instrument.
bad result: Empty duplicate, missing clips/device, or no new track.

prompt: Rename that copy to 'Keys 2'.
good result: Renamed.
bad result: Wrong track renamed, or no change.

prompt: Delete the Keys 2 track and the Vocals track, I don't need them.
good result: Both gone; the original three tracks intact.
bad result: Wrong track deleted (especially the original Keys), or one of the two left behind.

prompt: Delete the bass clip out of the Break scene, and then delete the Break scene entirely.
good result: Clip gone, then scene gone. Verse and Chorus untouched.
bad result: Wrong clip/scene deleted, or Verse/Chorus affected.

prompt: Where are we at — read me the grid and the session one more time.
good result: Three tracks, two scenes (Verse, Chorus), everything consistent with the screen — names, tempo 86, mix positions. This is the real test of state tracking after a messy session.
bad result: Any drift between the report and the screen: ghost tracks/scenes, stale tempo, wrong mix state.

========== PART 9 — WRAP UP ==========

prompt: Turn the metronome off and play me the Verse one last time.
good result: Metronome icon off, Verse scene plays.
bad result: Metronome stays on, or wrong scene plays.

prompt: Stop. That's a wrap.
good result: Stopped.
bad result: Keeps playing.

========== REPORTING ==========
For each failure note: which step, your exact words, what Seshat said (did it
claim success?), what Live actually did (including "nothing"), and which tool
was called if you can expand it.

Classify each as:
- Broken: Seshat claimed success, Live didn't change (usually a wrong OSC address).
- Wrong: something happened, but not what you asked (tool description / prompting problem).
- Judgement: it worked, but the musical result was poor. Note separately — these are prompt-quality signals only a human can report. Be opinionated.
